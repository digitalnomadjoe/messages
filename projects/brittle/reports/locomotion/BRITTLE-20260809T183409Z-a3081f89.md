## Decision

# `CADENCE CREDIT RCA INCONCLUSIVE`

**Smallest PPO repair (exactly one):**

> **`--gae-lambda 0.95 → 0.99`.** Nothing else. At γ = 0.99, λ = 0.95 the GAE effective credit
> horizon is `1/(1−γλ)` = **16.8 steps = 0.336 s**, while a commanded same-foot cadence interval
> is **1.00–1.43 s**. The credit path is **3.0–4.2× shorter than the quantity the cadence reward
> measures.** λ = 0.99 lifts the horizon to **50.3 steps = 1.005 s**, which spans a full interval
> at c = 1.00 and most of one at c = 0.85, and still fits inside the T = 64 (1.28 s) rollout.
> (λ = 0.995 would give 1.34 s, which exceeds the rollout and is therefore not realizable.)

**Bounded validation run:** branch from probe C `3a04b5e34d50230d`, native 22-member allocation,
fwd weight 12, cadence weight 19.5, `--timing-latent` OFF, **`--gae-lambda 0.99` the only change**,
**294,912 env steps / 2 iterations**, inspector throughout with exact-PID close, no continuation.
Judge on the probe-E/F four-clause gate — slope improves > 0.0394, max |v − v_cmd| ≤ 0.020,
3/3 survive 400 steps, plus the obs53 directional share as the mechanism read.

**Confidence: MEDIUM.** The horizon mismatch is arithmetic and certain; that it is *the* binding
constraint is a hypothesis this audit could not test, for the reason in §3.

## 2. Advantage-normalisation counterfactual

Mode A reproduces the trainer's own semantics exactly — `advm = (adv − μ_stratum)/σ_stratum` with
σ floored at 0.3× global (`train_tip_reftrack.py:5576-5578`, applied at `:5832`). No other PPO
semantics differ between modes.

| | **A** command-regime | **B** global | **C** none |
|---|---|---|---|
| aggregate ‖g‖ | 9.1478 | 9.4612 | 17.186 |
| between-command mean spread | 0.0459 | 0.1822 | 0.2090 |
| mean within-command sd | 1.0049 | 1.0382 | 1.1909 |
| **separation ratio** | **0.0457** | **0.1755** | **0.1755** |
| pairwise cadence cosine | +0.926 / +0.900 / +0.940 | +0.924 / +0.907 / +0.945 | +0.979 / +0.941 / +0.968 |
| **directional → base gait** | **96.5 %** | **96.5 %** | **96.2 %** |
| **directional → obs53** | **2.7 %** | **2.8 %** | **3.0 %** |
| directional → obs52 | 0.7 % | 0.7 % | 0.8 % |

**Hypothesis A is real but not the bottleneck.** Command-regime normalisation *does* remove
cadence-conditioned information — it cuts the separation ratio **3.8×**, from 0.1755 to 0.0457,
exactly as the hypothesis predicts. But restoring that structure buys almost nothing where it
matters: the command-conditioned share of the gradient moves **2.7 % → 3.0 %**, and the per-cadence
gradients become *slightly more* aligned (0.90–0.94 → 0.94–0.98), i.e. **less** command-distinct,
not more.

So the answer to "is normalisation removing the information?" is **yes** — and the answer to
"would preserving it materially increase command-conditioned gradient authority?" is **no**. The
enum requires the second, not the first.

## 3. Temporal-credit localisation

`p_cadence` advantages only, mode-A normalisation, bucketed by time since the nearest touchdown.

⚠ **A units correction I made mid-analysis:** `cad_since` holds **frames**, not seconds — the
trainer says so at `:799` and multiplies by 0.02 at `:1234`. My first bucketing treated frames as
seconds and put 93 % of samples in a spurious ">1 s" bucket. Corrected below; the first pass is
discarded, not reported.

| bucket (s) | n / cadence | ‖g‖ | mean pairwise cosine | → obs53 | SNR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.0 – 0.20** | 4057 | 1.3152e+02 | **+0.691** | 2.8 % | 1.1532 |
| **0.20 – 0.50** | 1470 | 1.1765e+02 | **+0.554** | 2.2 % | 1.2319 |
| 0.50 – 1.00 | 1 | — | — | — | — |
| > 1.00 | 0 | — | — | — | — |

**Hypothesis B predicts near-event gradients are substantially more command-distinct and
higher-SNR. They are neither.** The near bucket is **less** distinct (+0.691 vs +0.554 — higher
cosine means more alike) and **lower** SNR (1.1532 vs 1.2319). The trend runs the wrong way for B.

⚠ **And the test has a hard limit I cannot argue past.** At realized cadences of 1.18–1.87 Hz with
two alternating feet, the time since the *nearest* touchdown almost never exceeds 0.5 s — the
0.5–1.0 s bucket holds **one** sample and the > 1 s bucket holds **none** in a 64-step rollout. So
what I actually compared is **near versus mid (0–0.2 s vs 0.2–0.5 s)**, not near versus
long-horizon. B as literally stated is **not testable** on a T = 64 rollout. Within the range that
exists, its prediction is contradicted; beyond 0.5 s there is no evidence either way.

That limitation is itself why the verdict is `INCONCLUSIVE` rather than a clean rejection of B.

## 4. ⚠ What the measurement found that neither hypothesis named

The horizon arithmetic, which no normalisation or bucketing choice can change:

| | steps | seconds |
|---|---|---|
| GAE effective credit horizon, γ=0.99 λ=0.95 | **16.8** | **0.336** |
| rollout horizon T | 64 | 1.28 |
| commanded interval c = 1.00 | 50 | 1.000 — **3.0×** the horizon |
| commanded interval c = 0.85 | 59 | 1.176 — **3.5×** |
| commanded interval c = 0.70 | 71 | 1.429 — **4.2×**, and **longer than the whole rollout** |
| realized intervals at probe C | 27–42 | 0.533–0.847 — 1.6–2.5× |

**The cadence reward is defined over a same-foot interval that is 3–4× longer than the credit
path that has to explain it** — and at c = 0.70 the commanded interval does not even fit inside
one rollout. A reward whose defining event pair straddles the credit horizon cannot deliver
command-specific credit, however the advantages are normalised.

This is consistent with everything upstream and explains why nothing has moved: rows 125/126
showed the *actor* had no distinct direction and then that giving it one changed nothing; this
row shows the *credit path* is too short to carry the signal that would have driven it. It is a
hypothesis, not a proof — §3 could not test long horizons — which is why the repair is a single
free hyperparameter and a bounded run rather than a recommendation to spend the long budget.
