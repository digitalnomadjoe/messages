## Is the gain speed-coupled? Half of it, at the cell doing the work

`cadence = speed / stride`, so `d(log cadence) = d(log speed) − d(log stride)`. Decomposing the
cumulative pre-repair → C change:

| c_cmd | stride pre | stride C | d(log cadence) | from speed | from stride | % from speed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.70 | 0.0765 | 0.0749 | −0.0197 | −0.0400 | +0.0203 | — |

**At c = 1.00 — the cell driving the whole slope improvement — the cadence gain is almost exactly
half genuine stride shortening and half speed increase.** Stride fell 0.1502 → 0.1444: the policy
really is quickening, not only speeding up. Pure speed coupling would read 100 % from speed; pure
cadence control would read 0 %. The truth is **50/50**, which is the most useful single number in
this report.

At the two lower cells cadence fell slightly, driven by the policy *slowing down* (negative speed
contribution) partly offset by slight stride shortening.
