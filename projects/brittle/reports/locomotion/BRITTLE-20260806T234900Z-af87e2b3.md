## 1. Per-regime sample parity — exact

Q5A gave each of three regimes 1344 worlds × 64 steps × 8 rollouts. The controls reproduce that per regime with **nworld 2688 = 2 × 1344**:

| quantity | Q5A (3 members) | **Q6 control (2 members)** | match |
|---|---|---|---|
| worlds per regime | 1344 | **1344** | exact |
| samples per regime per rollout | 86,016 | **86,016** | exact |
| **samples per regime, total** | **688,128** | **688,128** | **exact** |
| optimizer epochs (rollouts) | 8 | **8** | exact |
| minibatches per epoch | 63 | 42 | — |
| **per-regime gradient weight per epoch** | 63 × ⅓ = **21.0** | 42 × ½ = **21.0** | **exact** |
| total steps | 2,064,384 | 1,376,256 | ≤ ceiling |

★ The gradient-weight row is the one that makes this a fair control. `--adv-norm-per-regime` computes the policy loss as an **equal-weight mean over the regimes present**, so a two-member run gives its turn ½ of the gradient instead of ⅓. Halving the world count restores the product exactly. **The naive same-total-step run would have given the surviving turn 1,032,192 samples (50 % more) and 31.5 gradient-weight units (50 % more)** — precisely the comparison the ticket rules out.

⚠ **One matched quantity is not exact, and it is disclosed:** the anchor weight decays with global steps, so over 1,376,256 steps it ends at **0.1254** versus Q5A's **0.1131** at the same flags. The controls therefore end *more* anchored than Q5A — a restraint on the turn, not an advantage — so it cannot manufacture a solo success. Flags were preserved verbatim rather than rescaled.

## 7. Why the decision is `LEFT ONLY CLOSES`

- `BOTH SOLO TURNS CLOSE` — false; right never clears its κ gate at any checkpoint, and its 32×5 CI is entirely on the failing side.
- `NEITHER SOLO TURN CLOSES` — false; left passes 12/12 and its 32×5 CI is entirely on the passing side.
- `RIGHT ONLY CLOSES` — false.
- `GUARD OR CONSTRUCTION FAILED` — false; both preflights passed, both windows and runs are receipted.
- `MULTIPLE BLOCKERS` — false.
- **`LEFT ONLY CLOSES`** — and per the ticket's interpretation table, that points at **side-specific dynamics, reward income, or contact asymmetry**, not at joint optimization of opposing turns.

**Per the ticket I do not infer a network-capacity limit**, and the evidence actively argues against one: left closes solo *and* survives as an OOD skill inside the right-only run, so the network plainly holds both turn behaviours at once — what it cannot do is push the *right* turn's stochastic magnitude past ≈ −0.93 under any configuration tried.

**Recommendation — the one probe this result authorizes.** Q6 removed opposition and the right deficit did not move, so the next question is *what is different about the right turn on this plant*. The smallest decisive measurement is a **side-asymmetry audit on the two members themselves, before any further training**: compare the kP200 and kM200 references and their realized executions for (i) per-side reward income decomposed by term at matched phase, (ii) contact/slip and support-margin statistics by stance leg, and (iii) the plant's own left/right symmetry under the mirror operator validated in P — the same operator that produced a working right specialist from the left one. If the reference or the income is asymmetric, that is the blocker and it is fixable upstream of PPO; if the plant is asymmetric, that is a plant finding and outranks everything in this arc. **Nothing is banked, promoted or pointed at.**
