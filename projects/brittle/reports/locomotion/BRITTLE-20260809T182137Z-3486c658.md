## ⚠ The finding: the pathway was used, but not for cadence

The latent trained — output layer **0 → 0.3858** — so PPO did put signal into the new subspace.
What it put there is the point:

| c_cmd | gain_s | gain_c | bias_s | bias_c |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.70 | 0.02493 | 0.04888 | −0.02862 | −0.02959 |
| 0.85 | 0.02733 | 0.05333 | −0.03135 | −0.03234 |

| **spread across the full cadence range** | **0.00476** | **0.00879** | **0.00541** | **0.00546** |

The FiLM is **monotone in cadence** — the direction is right — but the variation is tiny against
its own magnitude and against its bound:

* **28.8 %** of the ±0.2 authority is used **in total**
* only **4.4 %** of that authority is **command-conditioned** (max spread 0.00879 / 0.2)
* the command-conditioned part is **~15 %** of the latent's own output; **~85 % is a constant**
  applied identically at c = 0.70, 0.85 and 1.00

**Given a purpose-built command-conditioned pathway, PPO used it as another base-gait knob.**

## What this settles for the architecture review

Row 125 diagnosed `CADENCE REPRESENTATION ENTANGLEMENT`: the isolated cadence gradient spends
94–96 % of its effect on command-independent base gait because the actor has no direction that
expresses command conditioning. The obvious reading was *"give it one."* **We did, and it changed
nothing.**

That narrows the cause considerably. It is **not** that the actor lacked the capacity — the
subspace existed, was reachable, was used, and was bounded generously enough (28.8 % consumed, so
authority was not the constraint). The gradient simply did not ask for command differentiation
*even when a clean place to put it was available*.

That points upstream, to the credit signal, and it is consistent with the one number row 119
already measured and I did not weight heavily enough at the time: **the between-cadence advantage
separation was only ~10–12 % of the within-stratum noise** (0.097 at probe C's base, 0.119 after
884k). A pathway cannot be driven by a signal that is an order of magnitude below the noise it
competes with.

**My reading for your review, offered and not acted on:** the binding constraint is the
*advantage/credit* signal, not actor capacity. Further actor-side changes are unlikely to move
this. The two things that would actually test the upstream hypothesis are (a) per-command
advantage normalisation replaced by something that preserves rather than removes between-command
mean structure, or (b) a credit horizon short enough that a cadence error attributes to the steps
that caused it. Both are reward/optimiser-side, both are outside this ticket, and I have made
neither.
