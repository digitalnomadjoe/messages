## The intervention, and why it did the opposite

**Selection (offline, on the current fixed trajectories, before PPO).** The existing term is
peaked **income**, `r_fwd = w · exp(−((v − tgt)/0.08)²)`, so leakage costs *forgone* income.

| multiplier | w | worst-leakage cost | increase | dL/dv | r_fwd max income as % of positive sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| current | 12 | 0.5545 | — | 62.2 | 50.1 % |
| **1.25× (chosen)** | **15** | **0.6931** | **+25 %** | **77.8** | **55.6 %** |
| 1.5× | 18 | 0.8317 | +50 % | 93.4 | 60.1 % |
| 2.0× | 24 | 1.1089 | +100 % | 124.5 | 66.7 % |

I chose the **smallest**, because the subordination constraint binds from above, hard: `r_fwd` at
max income is **already 50.1 %** of the measured positive per-step sum, and 2.0× (24.0) would put
a single term above the *entire* measured sum (23.97). Verified live by dry read-back:
`fwd_dominant_w = 15.0`, reward contract `359915e1557a2d29 → 66a9e2b84c50f09d`.

**What happened instead:**

| | speed spread | Δ | max \|v − 0.153\| | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pre-repair | 0.0175 | — | 0.0108 | — |
| A | 0.0222 | +0.0047 | 0.0117 | +0.0009 |
| B | 0.0251 | +0.0029 | 0.0151 | +0.0034 |
| C | 0.0299 | +0.0048 | 0.0174 | +0.0023 |
| **D** (w = 15) | **0.0312** | **+0.0013** | **0.0248** | **+0.0074** |

**The spread barely moved (+0.0013, the smallest increment of the arc) while the absolute
deviation blew out (+0.0074, the largest).** That combination is diagnostic: all three cells rose
*together* — +0.0061 / +0.0071 / +0.0074 — a near-uniform upward speed shift. The spread is
insensitive to a common-mode shift; the ±0.020 band is not.

**Why strengthening the term produced this.** `r_fwd` peaks at **each member's own target vx**,
not at 0.153. The κ=0 members span vx ∈ {0.100, 0.107, 0.118, 0.130, 0.153, 0.168, 0.204, 0.240}.
Making a per-member peaked income *stronger* sharpens tracking toward each member's own target and
says nothing about where the gate's fixed-0.153 slice lands. The lever pulled was real, but it is
not the lever that holds this slice — so it bought sharper per-member speed tracking and let the
common-mode drift continue.

## ⚠ The finding that outlasts this probe

**The frozen −2.2 acceptance gate passed — `verdict: PASS`, `gate_slope: True`,
`gate_nominal_survival: True`, `gate_nominal_speed: True`, run `exit 0`** — the first `COMPLETE`
guarded run of this arc. And it passed *while* speed coupling was increasing, with the nominal
cell 0.0248 m/s off its command.

The gate's speed clause is `[0.12, 0.22]`, a band wide enough that 0.1778 sits comfortably inside
it. So **the 884K acceptance gate does not discriminate cadence control from speed coupling.**
Anything certified on that gate alone could be a policy that reaches the cadence slope by riding
speed. That is a gate-design finding, not a probe result, and it applies retroactively to how the
−2.2 bar should be read.

I am **not** proposing a gate change here — it is frozen by owner decision and this ticket does not
authorize touching it. I am flagging that a PASS on it should now be treated as necessary, not
sufficient.

## What I would do instead of raising the weight again

Stated as a recommendation, not acted on. The failure is **common-mode speed drift at a fixed
command**, and `r_fwd` cannot see it because it is centred per member. Two options that address the
actual mechanism:

1. **Hold the evaluation slice explicitly** — the gate cells are the only place we require
   v = 0.153; nothing in training does. A member-level fix (a κ=0 iso-speed triplet already exists
   at vx = 0.153) means the *reward* already has the information; what is missing is weight on it.
2. **Constrain common-mode, not spread** — the metric that caught this is max |v − v_cmd|, and the
   metric that missed it is spread. Any future speed guard should bound the deviation, not the
   dispersion.

Raising `fwd_dominant_w` again is the one thing the evidence argues against: it was tried at the
smallest permitted step and the leakage grew fastest under it.
