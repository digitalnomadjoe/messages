## 2. ⚠ Row 129's "clean monotone Pareto frontier" was a sampling artifact

Row 129 mined four checkpoints — 2,359,296 / 3,244,032 / 4,128,768 / 4,423,680 — and read a
monotone slope-vs-speed trade. **All four sit on the recovery limb.** The full curve is
**U-shaped in slope**:

- **0.29M → 2.06M:** slope *degrades* −2.0310 → **−2.3807**
- **2.06M → 4.42M:** slope *recovers* −2.3807 → −1.7975

Speed error, meanwhile, is flat-then-rising: it oscillates 0.0159–0.0238 through the first 2M with
no trend, then drifts out after ~2.4M and never returns.

So the correct statement is not "every slope gain cost speed." It is: **the run first got worse at
cadence, then bought its recovery with speed.** That correction matters because row 129's framing
implied the early checkpoints were the good ones on speed and the late ones good on slope; in fact
the best *slope* in band is the **earliest** checkpoint.

## 6. Verdict and recommendation — STOP

**No candidate is clean in-distribution. Per the ticket: STOP and return for the architecture
decision.** I am not recommending heldout qualification.

The case, stated plainly: heldout qualification measures generalisation across speed × cadence ×
curvature. This candidate does not track *either* commanded axis in-distribution — it realises
50 % of the commanded speed span and inverts the cadence command. A heldout sweep would measure
how well that blindness generalises, which is not a question worth the compute.

**What survived and is worth keeping:** survival is unconditional (22/22, 12/12 curved, 12/12
fresh-spawn) and torque is comfortable (54.6 % of the 12.5 Nm limit, better than the final
checkpoint). The failure is specific and it is command tracking, not stability.

⚠ **One bounded thing I did not do.** The ticket scoped the surface to the best candidate only, so
**`ppo_884736` was not surface-tested.** I expect it to land near 0.022 as well — the two
checkpoints measured so far sit at 0.0222 and 0.0220 despite fixed-v readings of 0.0195 and 0.0272,
which suggests surface speed error is roughly constant across this run and independent of the
fixed-v probe. That is an inference, not a measurement. If you want the STOP made airtight rather
than inferred, one more surface run on `884736` settles it; say the word and it is ~10 minutes.
