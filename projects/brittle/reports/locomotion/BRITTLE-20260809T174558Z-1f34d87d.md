## Why over-weighting the triplet lowered cadence

The decomposition is unambiguous — **stride lengthened at every cell**:

| c_cmd | stride C | stride E | d(log cadence) | from speed | from stride |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.70 | 0.0749 | **0.0762** | −0.0124 | +0.0038 | −0.0162 |
| 0.85 | 0.0944 | **0.1002** | −0.0371 | +0.0229 | −0.0600 |
| 1.00 | 0.1444 | **0.1571** | −0.0554 | +0.0291 | −0.0846 |

Cadence fell at all three cells, and in every one the **stride term dominates** the loss while
speed pushed the other way. So the policy did not slow down — it **took longer steps**.

**The mechanism is the triplet's own geometry.** `stride = vx / c`, so at the fixed vx = 0.153 the
triplet's reference strides are **0.2186 / 0.1800 / 0.1530** — the c=0.70 member carries the
*longest stride in the entire manifold* (family range 0.118–0.240). Putting 30 % of worlds on
these three shifts the imitation target toward **longer strides**, and longer strides at a given
speed *are* lower cadence. The intervention pulled the policy toward exactly the gait property we
are trying to move away from.

That is the trap in this repair: the triplet is the only place cadence is *identifiable*, but it
is also a long-stride corner of the manifold. Weighting it up buys identifiability and
simultaneously buys stride — and stride won.
