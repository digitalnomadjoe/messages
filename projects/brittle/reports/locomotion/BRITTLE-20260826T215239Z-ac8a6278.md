## 2 · The scoped change

The minimum change that makes divergence impossible is not to recompute the schedule at the wiring site — that creates a second copy of the law that can drift — but to push `sp_w` from the value the schedule has just produced:

```python
self.cadence_cmd = _c * np.ones_like(self.cadence_cmd)      # existing line, untouched
if getattr(self, "sched_tempo", False):                      # WALK-DR06H3A
    _prev = getattr(self, "_sched_sp_prev", None)
    if _prev is None or not np.array_equal(_prev, self.cadence_cmd):
        _spv = (1.0 / np.maximum(self.cadence_cmd, 1e-6)).astype(np.float32)
        self.sim.set_rp_step_period_w(_spv)
        self._sched_sp_prev = np.array(self.cadence_cmd, copy=True)
```

Change-guarded (compared by value, on a `copy=True` snapshot), so it is one GPU write per (re)schedule — with fixed `vx_cmd` it fires exactly once. **The legacy schedule is untouched.** The setup-time write is also left exactly as it was: correcting on the first phase advance is safe because the kernel cannot pay same-foot cadence income before then (it needs a real prior touchdown on that foot *and* a period ≥ 0.30 s = 15 policy steps). Verification added a second reason it is safe that I had not given: on that first step `cadence_track`'s `T_cmd` is *also* still `1/c_member`, so the pre-push step is not merely unpaid, it is non-divergent.

**Opt-in and fail-closed.** `--cadence-sched-tempo`, default OFF, refusing without `--cadence-schedule`, with `--f8-sched`, without `--cmd-phase`, and without a manifold. The card in turn **requires** the flag. Refusals proven by injection:

**Two defects of my own, both caught by verification, both fixed.**

1. **A dead fail-closed guard.** My first F8 check tested `getattr(args, "f8", False) or getattr(env, "_f8", False)`. There is no `--f8` dest, and `env._f8` is not set until `wire_f8_sched()` runs *below* the guard — so it could never fire. It now tests `args.f8_sched`, and the proof binds to that dest and to the line ordering rather than to the message text.
2. **A receipt that could lie.** `env.sched_tempo` was set only inside the manifold branch, and nothing required `--cmd-phase`. A run with the flag but no manifold, or no `--cmd-phase`, would have accepted it, never pushed `sp_w`, and still emitted `sp_w_binds_to: "SCHEDULED"` and `timing_coherent: true`. `resolved_config` is written at setup and cannot observe whether the push later happened, so the only sound fix is to make the preconditions airtight at setup — which is what the four refusals above now do. ## 5 · Magnitude — corrected, and what it means

Per policy step at the ratchet member. `cadence_track` enters `out["reward"]` with **no** `dt` and is held between events; the kernel total is multiplied by `dt` (`kernels_tip.py:1220`), and its per-event payout `0.5·T·K` against `2·dt/T` events per step gives an average of `w·K·dt²`.

| same-foot period | `cadence_track` | kernel S6 | ratio | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0000 s | −0.0000 | +0.000339 | — | scheduled target / H3 goal |
| 1.2040 s | −0.4058 | +0.000896 | 453 : 1 | DR06H2 realized (0.8306 Hz) |
| 1.3933 s | −1.3080 | +0.001200 | 1090 : 1 | the stale S6 target |

Maxima: kernel S6 **0.0012/step** against `cadence_track`'s **9.14/step** — **~7,600 : 1**.

**Two corrections that both cut against my earlier number.** The card passes `rp_cadence_rew_w=1.5`, but the trainer appends `rp_cadence_rew_w=3.0` unconditionally at `:4831`, immediately before `build_sim`, and the `tip_weight` merge is last-wins — so the runtime weight is **3.0**, and my report's 1.5 was wrong. Against that, the kernel's `dt` scaling makes the per-step figure `w·K·dt² = 0.0012`, not the `w·dt = 0.03` I quoted, which had mixed a per-second kernel figure against a per-step env figure. Net: the kernel term is ~25× smaller than I said.

**And the stale bias was mixed in direction** — across the 23 members, 7 were biased slower than scheduled and **15 faster**, 1 unaffected. A defect that pushes both ways cannot produce the uniform ~0.83-of-commanded ceiling seen in DR06H2.

So: the stale target was a genuine second authority pointing the wrong way at the ratchet member, and running PPO with two contradictory gait-period targets would have made any result uninterpretable — which is exactly why you asked for this first. But **it was never strong enough to hold the gait**, and the ceiling remains unexplained. It sits alongside the WALK-DR06H3 finding that `r_fwd` is simultaneously leaving ~10.6 of 12 income unclaimed: two independent reward channels are both paying for more speed and more cadence than the policy delivers.
