## 1. Exact state variables and update equations

Per world, per foot `f ∈ {R, L}`; `T_cmd = 1/c_cmd` (per world), `dt = 0.02`,
`δ = 0.25`, `CAP = 0.4688`, `STALL_MULT = 3.0`.

```
state:  td_last[f] : int32    policy-step index of the last touchdown, -1 if none yet
        rho[f]     : float32  held bounded penalty, init 0.0
        prev[f]    : bool     previous `loaded` flag, for the rising edge

every policy step t:
    rise = loaded[f] & ~prev[f]

    if rise and td_last[f] >= 0:                    # a COMPLETE same-foot interval closed
        e      = ((t - td_last[f])*dt - T_cmd) / T_cmd
        rho[f] = min( huber_0.25(e), CAP )

    if rise:
        td_last[f] = t

    since = (t - td_last[f])*dt   if td_last[f] >= 0   else   t*dt
    if since > STALL_MULT * T_cmd:                  # stalled foot
        rho[f] = CAP

    prev[f] = loaded[f]

    p_cadence(t) = -( rho[R] + rho[L] ) / 2         # applied EVERY step
```

**Holding the value between events is the whole mechanism.** A value held for an episode
contributes the same whether it was refreshed 8 times or 16, which is precisely what removes
the dependence on touchdown count.

## 2. Count-independence — exact, and the residual is attributed

Two synthetic worlds, identical normalised error `e = −0.5` (each stepping exactly twice as
fast as commanded), differing only in commanded cadence:

| | touchdown events | **naive** per-event total | **online** per-step mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| `c_cmd = 0.50`, realized 1.0 Hz | 14 | −1.219 | −0.07910 |
| `c_cmd = 1.00`, realized 2.0 Hz | 30 | −2.719 | −0.08648 |
| **ratio** | 2.14× | **2.23×** | **0.91×** |

The naive form very nearly doubles for the same relative error. The online form does not — and
the remaining 9 % is **attributed, not waved at**:

| episode | full-episode ratio | post-startup ratio | startup steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| 400 steps | 0.9146 | **1.0000** | 75 / 37 |
| 4000 steps | 0.9921 | **1.0000** | 75 / 37 |

Excluding the pre-first-interval window the two means are **−0.09375 and −0.09375 — identical**.
The full-episode gap shrinks from 8.5 % to 0.8 % as the episode lengthens. **The residual is the
startup window; count dependence is exactly zero.**

**Absolute divergence |online − offline|: max 0.01909 = 4.1 % of the cap.** Ratios span
0.851–3.48, but every large ratio sits at `c = 1.00` where *both* numbers are near zero

term the absolute gap is what matters.

The two differ by construction and the difference is principled: the online form is a
**time-weighted** mean of the same bounded ρ values, the offline was **interval-count-weighted**.
Where they diverge, time-weighting gives a long bad interval more influence — the more faithful
reading of *how long was the robot mis-paced*. Ordering across cadence strata is preserved in
both: 0.70 > 0.85 > 1.00 in magnitude, seed and CY alike.

Standing and one-foot hold sit at the cap after the 3·`T_cmd` grace, so their per-step mean is

The grace fires **once per foot per episode** — it is gated on "no complete interval yet", so it
cannot be re-farmed by repeatedly stopping.

## 6. Recalibrated weight — 17.5 → **19.5**

The ledger terms are **per-step means** — they sum to the per-step return
(`base + r_zt + track + r_yaw + r_fwd + p_slip + r_lift + p_cap + p_flat = 4.199` against
`mean_ep_ret = 4.26`), so the online per-step term is directly comparable to them.

Same rule as row 111, stated before the number: **the worst cell must cost no more than
`track_feet`**.

```
worst ONLINE per-step |p_cadence| over all 18 cells = 0.11520
w = 2.2411 / 0.11520 = 19.45  ->  w = 19.5        (row 111's offline-derived value: 17.5)
```

| cell | online/step | w·p | % of positive sum | % of fall cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| worst overall (SEED κ+0.60, c=0.70) | 0.11520 | **2.2464** | **9.37 %** | 4.49 % |
| CY straight c=0.70 | 0.10741 | 2.0944 | 8.74 % | 4.19 % |
| CY straight c=0.85 | 0.07532 | 1.4687 | 6.13 % | 2.94 % |
| CY straight c=1.00 | 0.01016 | 0.1982 | 0.83 % | 0.40 % |

Worst cell = **69.2 % of `track_jnt`**, 9.37 % of the positive per-step sum, 4.49 % of the fall
cost. Meaningful, and subordinate to the walking basin on every comparison the ticket named.

## 7. ⚠ One material caveat: the term is weakest when episodes are shortest

The signal only exists after a foot's **first complete interval**. At `c = 1.00` with realized
~1.07 Hz that is ~47 steps; at `c = 0.70` with realized ~1.9 Hz it is ~26 steps.

Against the acquisition's own episode lengths — **37.9 → 67.5 → 82.4** steps across the three
checkpoints — this means:

* at the **first** checkpoint (37.9 steps) a `c = 1.00` world would frequently close **no**
  interval at all and contribute **zero** cadence signal;
* by the **last** (82.4 steps) a `c = 1.00` world carries signal for ~43 % of the episode, and a
  `c = 0.70` world for ~68 %.

**The inverted cells produce signal soonest** — they step fastest, so they close intervals
earliest — which is favourable: the cells needing correction are the best served. But early
training is carried by survival and the imitation anchor, not by this term. That is a reason to
expect slow initial movement, and it is exactly why the sign-check is placed after episodes have
had a chance to lengthen rather than at the first checkpoint.
