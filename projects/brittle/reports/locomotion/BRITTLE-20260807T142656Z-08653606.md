## 0. Headline

**The mirror defect was real, it was worse than "wrong signs on one field", and correcting it makes the manifold better — it did not cost anything.**

Two findings, both measured on 7,680 **real** rollout states:

1. **The deployed P29 mirror had the sign exactly inverted on two of the three angular-velocity dims** for this checkpoint's actual contract — 200.03 % and 199.86 % relative residual against the true mirrored-state observation. Not a subtle mismatch: a sign flip.
2. **No correct signed-permutation mirror exists for the v1 contract at all.** The best-fit *linear* 3×3 map on the angular-velocity block still leaves **10.95 %** residual, so "the physically correct v1 mirror" is not a thing that can be constructed. The corrected `M_v1` repairs dims 3 and 4 and leaves dim 5 as irreducibly wrong as it already was.

And the A/B says the correction is free-or-better: **Q10's one real blemish, the mid-range left bias, largely disappears** (32×5 mirror offset at \|F\|=0.5: **+0.0761 → +0.0025** det, **+0.0611 → +0.0004** stoch), stochastic falls drop **3/800 → 1/800**, and torque is unchanged.

## 1. Part A — what the mirror must be, established by experiment

Everything reduces to one premise that `mirror_contract.py` asserts in a docstring and that nothing had verified: **which frame is MuJoCo's free-joint `qvel[3:6]`?** If it is body-frame then `v1 = Rᵀ·qvel[3:6]` is a double rotation; if it is world-frame the v1/v2 sign assignments *swap*.

| step | method | result |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** frame of `qvel[3:6]` | controlled integration test, 6 random (R, ω) pairs, gravity off: compare `R(t+dt)` against `exp([ω]×dt)·R` (world) and `R·exp([ω]×dt)` (body) | **BODY-frame** — err **2.0e-06** vs **1.07e-03** |
| **A2** is this checkpoint really v1? | reimplement `tip_observation_kernel` dims 0-32 in numpy, compare to the live sim on real states | **MATCH, max \|err\| 4.20e-07** — the contract is `obs[3:6] = Rᵀ qvel[3:6]`, the double rotation |
| **A3** what does the mirror actually do? | mirror 7,680 real states with the canonical `mirror_state`, recompute the observation, compare field by field | table below |

**Field-by-field residual — predicted mirror vs the observation of the truly mirrored state:**

| dim | field | **V2 (P29, deployed)** | **V1 (legacy)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 | projected gravity x/y/z | **0.00 %** | **0.00 %** |
| **3** | body angvel x (`Rᵀqvel`) | **200.03 %** | **1.57 %** |
| **4** | body angvel y | **199.86 %** | **5.24 %** |
| **5** | body angvel z | **87.58 %** | **87.58 %** |
| 6-8 | body linear velocity x/y/z | **0.00 %** | **0.00 %** |
| 9-32 | joint q and qd blocks (24 dims) | **0.0000 %** | **0.0000 %** |

★ **Gravity, linear velocity and all 24 joint dims mirror exactly under both candidates.** The entire dispute is three dims, and P29 had two of them sign-inverted.

★★ **A signed-permutation mirror for v1 cannot exist, and this is provable rather than empirical bad luck.** With `ω' = −M_L ω` and `R' = M_W R M_L`, the v1 field maps as `u' = −M_L Rᵀ (M_W M_L) ω` — the extra `diag(−1,−1,1)` sits *between* `Rᵀ` and `ω`, so `u'` depends on the robot's orientation and is not a fixed linear function of `u`. The measurement agrees: the best-fit 3×3 leaves 10.95 % residual, and its third row is `[+0.028, −0.219, −0.486]` — nothing like ±1. **`NEG_OBS_V1`'s dim-5 entry is a best-fit, exactly as `mirror_contract.py` warns, and it is 87.58 % wrong either way.**

**So "correct the mirror" means: repair the two dims that are repairable (3, 4) and leave dim 5 as-is.** `M_v1` is built as a **minimal edit of the deployed matrix** — only the dim-3 and dim-4 diagonal signs change (+1→−1 and −1→+1). Every other entry, the whole command tail included, is byte-identical to `P29_Mobs`. Involution verified exact; negated diagonal set is `{0, 3, 5, 6, 45}` = `NEG_OBS_V1`. `g_v1` equivariance residual w.r.t. `M_v1`: **0.000e+00**.

## 3. Q10's finding stands

The ticket asks explicitly that Q10 not be invalidated, and it is not. **Q10 measured `g_old` and found it interpolates; that measurement remains true and this unit reproduces it.** What Q10A adds is that the wrapper Q10 measured was not the physically-consistent one, and the physically-better one **interpolates at least as well and is measurably more symmetric**. The continuous mapping is a property of the underlying checkpoint, not of which of these two wrappers is bolted on.

⚠ **Worth stating for the record:** the underlying `f` was *trained* with the P29/v2 mirror in the loop (Q8B/Q8C), so `f`'s weights were shaped around `g_old`. That `g_v1` nevertheless improves symmetry suggests the training benefit came from **exact equivariance with respect to a fixed involution** — which both wrappers provide — rather than from that involution being the physical mirror.

## 4. Why the decision

`g_v1` retains the continuous manifold with **no material regression on any measured axis** and improves mirror symmetry, straight-line bias, stochastic falls and stochastic monotonicity, at identical torque. That is the condition the ticket sets for `CONTINUOUS MANIFOLD CONTRACT-CORRECT — READY FOR VISUAL REVIEW`. `MIRROR FIX CHANGES BEHAVIOR — OWNER DECISION REQUIRED` would be the call if the correction had degraded the manifold; it did not.

⚠ **One caveat on the words "contract-correct".** `M_v1` is the **best available signed-permutation** mirror for the v1 contract, not an exact one: dim 5 remains 87.58 % wrong because, as §1 proves, no fixed linear map can mirror that field. If an exactly-mirrorable contract is wanted, that means moving the observation to **v2 semantics** (raw body rates) and retraining — a PPO decision this ticket does not authorize and I have not taken.
