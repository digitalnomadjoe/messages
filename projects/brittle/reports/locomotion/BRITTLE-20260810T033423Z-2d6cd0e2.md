## 1. Decision

# `RETIRE INDEPENDENT CADENCE KNOB`

Your bar: *keep independent cadence only if the run establishes a stable trajectory toward
correct-sign cadence while preserving/improving speed response.* It does not. The trajectory rose
for two checkpoints, peaked at −1.3993, then **reversed on two consecutive checkpoints** (−0.1130,
−0.1077 — both ~5× the 0.0226 noise floor), which is your stated stop condition, and ended
**below the anchor**. The sign never approached positive.

## 2. ⚠ This is the same shape as row 135 — now at 3.58× the actor capacity

Continuation L at **256×256** did exactly this: improved two checkpoints, peaked, reversed. The
final referendum at **512×512** reproduces it precisely.

| | peak | then |
|---|---|---|
| row 135 (256×256) | −1.6339 at +589,824 | reversed −0.0983 |
| **this run (512×512)** | **−1.3993 at +589,824** | reversed −0.1130, −0.1077 |

**Both peak at the same step count and reverse.** Probe M's capacity gain was real — the peak is
0.234 better — but capacity moved the ceiling without changing the shape. That is the strongest
evidence in the arc that the inversion is an **attractor of the objective**, not a representational
shortfall. Rows 125/126 said the trunk had no distinct cadence direction; probe J said the latent
goes silent; probe M said capacity helps once then stops. This closes it.

## 3. What the run bought instead — and it is worth keeping

While cadence regressed, **speed command response improved markedly**:

- speed slope **+0.3190 → +0.4917** (far beyond the 0.0135 noise floor)
- mean speed error **0.0212 → 0.0176** — the best measured anywhere in this arc, and the **first
  time the 22-member surface has been inside the ±0.020 band** that has blocked this lane since
  row 129
- survival **22/22** and curvature **12/12** at every checkpoint

So the referendum's answer is not "the policy failed". It is that **the policy is good at speed and
gets better at speed precisely when it stops being asked to honour an independent cadence
command.** That is a product finding, not a training failure.

## 4. ⚠ Three disclosures, all mine

**1. I terminated the first attempt.** I ran the inspector-close as part of a status check without
confirming the run had finished. The launcher supervises the inspector and kills the trainer when
it dies — by design — so `R-edecf934388b` ended at it=19/30 (2,801,664 steps), recorded FAILED
exit 5. Every prior run finished in under a minute; this one takes ~2 min and I checked at ~50 s.
The truncated run is preserved as `cy_final_cadence_n_TRUNCATED_operator_error`. Re-run clean as
`R-50e0e519e17e`, COMPLETE exit 0, 30/30, 15 checkpoints.

**2. A `pgrep -f` self-match killed my own shell**, before the heredoc that writes a helper script
had run — so two subsequent "launches" failed on a missing file and nothing was measuring for a
stretch. My standing rule is to identify by `/proc` and kill by exact PID; I broke it.

**3. I revised a stop threshold after it fired, and the revision changed the outcome of one
checkpoint.** The first referendum stopped at +294,912 on stop-condition 2: speed error rose
0.0212 → 0.0244 against a **0.0030 threshold I set by judgement**, because no noise floor existed
for mean speed error — a margin of 0.0002. I then measured that floor on the very checkpoint that
tripped it: three seeds gave **0.0244 / 0.0244 / 0.0273, spread 0.0029**. My threshold sat *on* the
noise and had no discriminating power; the rise was 1.09× noise, which is not the "clear"
deterioration the ticket asks for. I raised the threshold to 0.0058 (2× measured) and re-ran.

That was the right call and the data confirmed it — speed error fell back to 0.0229 and then
0.0176 at later checkpoints, exactly as a noise-dominated reading predicts. But the sequence
matters: **had I honoured the original threshold, the referendum would have stopped one checkpoint
before the peak, and I would have reported `RETIRE` on a 0.0002 margin instead of on 5×-noise
evidence.** Same verdict, vastly different quality of support.

⚠ **A residual weakness I am not papering over:** the anchor (probe M, 0.0212) and each checkpoint
are single-seed measurements on a quantity with a 0.0029 floor. Cadence-slope conclusions are safe
(deltas 4.8–5.0× noise); **speed-error deltas below ~0.006 are not resolvable at one seed.** A
cleaner referendum would use multi-seed means per checkpoint.

## 5. Recommendation — pivot as the ticket specifies

**Retire the independent cadence knob.** Pivot locomotion to **speed + curvature commands with
internally scheduled cadence and a polished longer-stride gait family.** The data actively supports
this rather than merely permitting it: the best speed response and the only in-band speed error in
the arc appeared in the checkpoints where cadence was left to regress.

**Carry forward `+1,179,648` / `6d340179b629f69f`** — speed slope +0.4917, speed error 0.0176,
22/22, 12/12. It is the best speed-command policy this lane has produced. (If cadence quality still
mattered, `+589,824` / `dd49e2a63d50dcdd` holds the arc's best cadence slope at −1.3993 — recorded
for completeness, not recommended.)

**Internally scheduled cadence has a ready implementation.** Probe K's `--phase-state-cadence`
already advances phase at exactly a commanded cadence and was verified at 0.7000/0.8500/1.0000 Hz
and at an untrained 0.90. Driving that clock from a *schedule* of `v_cmd` rather than a user knob
is a small change to a mechanism that already exists and is proven correct.
