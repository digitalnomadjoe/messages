## 4. Reading the failure honestly

Two facts sit together: the network *does* differentiate the commands at matched states, and the closed-loop behaviour on both turn commands degenerates toward straight — the one skill whose states dominate the data. The collection was driven by **L2-final**, the straight walker, so every state in all three sets is a straight-walker state; the turn teachers were queried where they themselves are partly off-distribution, and the student never sees the states a committed turn passes through. That is the O2 covariate-shift signature reappearing at manifold scale, and it is testable: the fix is per-command student-driven collection where each command is driven by a policy that can already execute it (the specialists), or an iterated DAgger whose later rounds are driven by the student on each command separately. Both are one bounded closure each and neither needs PPO.

I am **not** claiming direct distillation cannot work — only that one bounded closure with straight-driven data does not preserve the turns, and that the loss is toward the majority skill rather than a dead command channel.
