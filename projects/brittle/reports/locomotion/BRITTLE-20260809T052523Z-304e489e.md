### ⚠ Correction to row 113

I wrote: *"The grace fires once per foot per episode — it is gated on 'no complete interval yet',
so it cannot be re-farmed by repeatedly stopping."* **The second half of that is wrong.** Only the
*pre-first-interval* grace is once-per-episode. **The stall threshold is re-enterable:** once
touchdowns resume, `cad_since` resets, and a later stop gets another `3·T_cmd` before the cap
re-applies, holding the previous ρ meanwhile.

Measured, rather than restated:

| stop-and-tap (walk one cycle, stand H s, repeat) | ×19.5 | vs standing | moving |
|---|---|---|---|
| H = 0.5 s | 1.7253 | **79.6 % cheaper** | 68 % |
| H = 1.0 s | 3.9724 | 53.0 % cheaper | 50 % |
| H = 2.0 s | 8.2837 | 2.0 % cheaper | 35 % |
| H = 3.0 s | 8.2791 | 2.1 % cheaper | 25 % |
| H = 4.0 s | 8.2791 | 2.1 % cheaper | 20 % |

**Red's "~19 % cheaper" is not a single number** — the discount depends entirely on the hold
length, from 2 % at long holds to 79.6 % at short ones. I report the sweep rather than adopt a
figure I could not reproduce.

**What the numbers do support:** the discount tracks *how much the robot is actually walking* —
the 79.6 % case is moving 68 % of the time, which is most of a gait. No pattern buys ρ≈0 while
barely moving, because the interval that closes after a long hold is itself capped.

**What they do not support** is any claim that the term is exploit-proof. The honest statement is
economic, not structural: the most a policy can save by not walking is **8.46/step**, against
**+23.97/step** of positive reward it forfeits by not walking. Not advantageous — but that is an
argument about the *rest* of the reward, not a property of this term alone.
